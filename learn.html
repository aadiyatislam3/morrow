LEARN AT YOUR OWN PACE

Put words to common experiences.

These short guides are educational, not diagnostic. Open a topic to see what it can feel like, what daily life may be affected, and a few gentle ideas to try.

Stress and pressure

Pressure can build when responsibilities, expectations or uncertainty feel like too much at once.

Common experiences

  • Racing thoughts or feeling tense
  • Difficulty concentrating or getting started

Everyday impact

It may affect schoolwork, rest, patience or time with other people.

Things to try

  • Break one large task into a next small step.
  • Take a short pause for water, movement or slow breathing.

When reaching out may help

If pressure continues or starts affecting daily life, speaking with a trusted adult or counsellor may help.

Reliable sources

Further reading: WHO, Adolescent mental health

Anxiety and worry

Some teenagers experience worry that feels persistent, physical or difficult to switch off.

Common experiences

  • What-if thoughts, restlessness or a tight feeling
  • Wanting to avoid situations that feel uncertain

Everyday impact

Worry can make focus, sleep, decisions or social moments feel more effortful.

Things to try

  • Name the next thing you can control, rather than solving everything at once.
  • Try a slow exhale and return attention to your surroundings.

When reaching out may help

If worry feels overwhelming or limits activities, consider talking with someone you trust.

Reliable sources

Further reading: WHO, Adolescent mental health

Low mood

There are times when a person may feel down, flat or less hopeful than usual.

Common experiences

  • Low energy, tearfulness or feeling irritable
  • Finding ordinary tasks harder than they used to be

Everyday impact

It may change motivation, concentration, routines or interest in connecting.

Things to try

  • Keep one manageable routine anchor, such as a meal or shower.
  • Choose a small activity that usually gives a sense of comfort.

When reaching out may help

If low feelings continue or affect daily life, reach out to a trusted adult or professional.

Reliable sources

Further reading: WHO, Adolescent mental health

Sleep difficulties

Sleep can become difficult when routines, thoughts, stress or changing schedules get in the way.

Common experiences

  • Taking a long time to fall asleep or waking often
  • Feeling tired, foggy or irritable the next day

Everyday impact

Tiredness can make focus, mood and coping with ordinary tasks harder.

Things to try

  • Keep a consistent sleep and wake time where possible.
  • Create a short, lower-stimulation wind-down routine.

When reaching out may help

If sleep problems persist or make daily life difficult, consider speaking with a health professional.

Reliable sources

Further reading: WHO, Adolescent mental health

Body image and self-esteem

Many teenagers have moments of comparing themselves or feeling unsure about how they look or fit in.

Common experiences

  • Comparing your body or life with images online
  • Self-critical thoughts or avoiding photos and activities

Everyday impact

These feelings can affect confidence, eating patterns, clothing choices or social plans.

Things to try

  • Curate feeds that leave you feeling grounded rather than judged.
  • Notice what your body helps you do, not only how it appears.

When reaching out may help

If body worries change eating, movement or daily life, talk with a trusted adult or professional.

Reliable sources

Further reading: WHO, Adolescent mental health

SUPPORT MATTERS

You do not have to work it out alone.

A trusted adult, school counsellor, doctor or local support service can help you think through what is happening.

16263 - 24-hour government health service hotline. Official health service page

999 - emergency services. Government source

333 - government information and services. Government source

These are general services, not dedicated teen mental-health crisis lines. If you are in immediate danger, call 999 now.

SOURCES & FURTHER READING

Where the numbers come from.

These source notes make the meaning and limits of each statistic transparent. Accessed 11 September 2026.

Bangladesh school-based evidence

In a cross-sectional study of 563 school-going students aged 13-18 in selected schools in Dhaka City, 26.5% had moderate-to-severe depression and 18.1% had moderate-to-severe anxiety. The survey took place from November 2019 to February 2020 and was published in 2021. These findings are not a national estimate and the screening measures do not establish a clinical diagnosis.

Islam et al., PLOS ONE, 2021

Overall adolescent mental health

1 in 7, or 14.3%, of adolescents aged 10-19 experience a mental health condition globally. WHO also describes this as 15% of the global burden of disease in this age group. These are different measures and should not be treated as the same percentage.

World Health Organization, Adolescent mental health, updated 1 September 2025

Anxiety and depressive disorders

WHO estimates anxiety disorders among 4.1% of 10-14-year-olds and 5.3% of 15-19-year-olds. Depressive disorders are estimated among 1.3% of 10-14-year-olds and 3.4% of 15-19-year-olds. These are disorder estimates, not measures of ordinary worry or temporary sadness.

World Health Organization, Adolescent mental health, updated 1 September 2025

Suicide and age group

Suicide is the third leading cause of death among people aged 15-29. WHO also reports 727,000 suicides every year, a figure for all ages, so it is not presented here as an adolescent total.

World Health Organization, Suicide, updated 28 August 2026

Bangladesh context

WHO Bangladesh reports that more than 1 in 10 children in Bangladesh live with a mental disorder and that more than 90% of those who need care do not receive treatment. The page does not define “children” as adolescents, so this is national context, not adolescent-only prevalence.

WHO Bangladesh, Breaking the silence, published 24 August 2026

WHO does not provide a current global adolescent-only treatment-gap percentage or a single general age-of-onset percentage on the reviewed pages. Those figures are intentionally not added.
